Curbing aggregates private residential gardens into measurable urban biodiversity infrastructure.

By applying consistent ecological standards to residential land, Curbing transforms distributed private gardens into a verifiable environmental asset class for local government reporting and climate adaptation.

The Problem

Councils face mounting pressure to address urban heat, restore canopy cover, and meet biodiversity targets, yet public land represents a small fraction of total urban area. Reporting requirements demand measurable outcomes, but the infrastructure needed—parks, reserves, street trees—cannot scale quickly enough on public land alone.

Meanwhile, tree canopy continues to decline in Australian cities, urban heat intensifies, and habitat fragmentation worsens. Traditional approaches are constrained by budget limitations, land availability, and the long timeframes required for ecological restoration on public parcels.

The Unlock

Private residential land represents the majority of urban area and holds significant ecological potential, yet remains largely invisible to environmental planning and reporting frameworks. Most residential gardens already contain vegetation, but lack ecological coherence or measurable contribution to broader environmental goals.

Curbing treats private gardens as distributed environmental infrastructure. Through standardised design protocols, verified implementation, and aggregated measurement, residential parcels become legible contributors to urban biodiversity, canopy cover, and climate adaptation—enabling councils to leverage an existing, distributed asset at scale.

Why Now

  • Nature-positive commitments require measurable, verifiable outcomes that extend beyond traditional conservation approaches and public land portfolios.
  • Climate adaptation strategies increasingly recognise the role of distributed green infrastructure in addressing urban heat and stormwater management.
  • Reporting frameworks now demand granular environmental data, creating pressure to account for private land contributions that were previously unmeasured.
  • Residential engagement with ecological practices has matured, with growing awareness of native planting, biodiversity value, and climate impacts at the household level.
  • Aggregating distributed private actions offers a cost-effective scaling mechanism compared to acquiring and managing additional public land parcels.

Founders

Curbing was established by Tyson and Natasha, partners in Gardener & Son, an ecological garden design studio based in Melbourne. Their work focuses on residential implementation of ecological vegetation communities and native plant systems.

The approach reflects a systems orientation and a long time horizon. Environmental infrastructure develops over years and decades. Private land, properly structured, can serve as a durable component of urban ecological function.
